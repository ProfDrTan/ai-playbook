4Correctness: with the same key on both sides, decryption undoes encryption.\(\forall k,\forall m:\ D_k(E_k(m)) = m\)
For every key k and every message m, decrypting with k what was encrypted with k gives back m.

4Brute force: on average you search half the keyspace. DES has 256 keys.\(\mathbb{E}[\text{tries}] = \tfrac{|\mathcal{K}|}{2} = 2^{n-1}\)
The expected number of tries is the size of the keyspace over two, which is two to the n minus one.

4Reusing a stream key cancels the keystream and leaks both messages.\(c \oplus c' = (m \oplus z) \oplus (m' \oplus z) = m \oplus m'\)
c XOR c-prime equals m XOR m-prime: the keystream z cancels out.

4CTR mode turns a block cipher into a stream cipher (so does CFB).\(C_i = P_i \oplus E_k(\text{ctr}_i)\)
Ciphertext block i equals plaintext block i XOR the encryption of counter i.
W4-2
5Successful decryption does not prove the message is unaltered.\(D_k(c') \text{ decrypts} \;\not\Rightarrow\; m' = m\)
That c-prime decrypts does not imply that m-prime equals m.

5Two tag types: a hash has one input; a MAC has two (key + message).\(MD = H(M) \quad\text{vs}\quad T = F(K, M)\)
The digest equals H of M, versus the tag equals F of K and M.

5Any length in, fixed length out — so many inputs share an output: reversible in principle, infeasible in practice.\(H: \{0,1\}^{*} \rightarrow \{0,1\}^{n}\)
H maps bit strings of any length to bit strings of length n.

h equals B-one XOR B-two XOR … B-m; and B-one XOR B-two equals B-two XOR B-one.

5Birthday paradox: finding any collision costs far less than matching a given hash.\(1 - \prod_{i=0}^{22}\tfrac{365-i}{365} \approx 0.507;\quad 2^{n/2} \ll 2^{n}\)
One minus the product, for i from 0 to 22, of 365 minus i over 365, is about 0.507; and two to the n over two is far smaller than two to the n.

5HMAC: hash twice, with the key disguised two different ways.\(H\big((K^{+}\!\oplus \text{opad}) \,\|\, H((K^{+}\!\oplus \text{ipad}) \,\|\, M)\big)\)
H of: K-plus XOR opad, joined to H of K-plus XOR ipad joined to M.

5Diffie-Hellman: both sides reach the same key without sending it — but nothing proves who is on the other end.\(K = (\alpha^{b})^{a} = (\alpha^{a})^{b} = \alpha^{ab} \bmod q\)
K equals alpha to the b, raised to the a, equals alpha to the a, raised to the b, equals alpha to the a-b, mod q.

5RSA: e and d are inverses modulo φ(n). Example: 7 × 23 = 161 ≡ 1 (mod 160).\(ed \equiv 1 \pmod{\varphi(n)},\quad C = M^{e},\ M = C^{d} \bmod n\)
e times d is congruent to 1 modulo phi of n; C equals M to the e, and M equals C to the d, mod n.

5Digital signature: sign the hash with the private key; anyone checks with the public key.\(S = H(M)^{d} \bmod n,\quad \text{accept iff } S^{e} \bmod n = H(M)\)
S equals H of M to the d, mod n; accept if and only if S to the e, mod n, equals H of M.
