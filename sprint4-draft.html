2c · Mini tutorial: reading the numbers and making your call

What is "gold futures (/GC)"?

A futures contract is an agreement to buy or sell something at a set price on a future date. /GC is the COMEX gold futures contract. Its price is quoted in US dollars per troy ounce. You do not trade it; you only read its price and predict which way it moves.

Decoding the symbol GCZ26.CMX

PartMeans
GCGold futures
ZDelivery month, December (futures month letters: G = Feb, J = Apr, M = Jun, Q = Aug, V = Oct, Z = Dec)
26Year 2026
.CMXThe COMEX exchange

Why a named contract? Every month has its own contract with its own price. If you compare a price from one contract with a price from another, the gap between them can look like a market move when it is not. So everyone uses GCZ26.CMX for both dates. Yahoo's GC=F is a continuous symbol for the same product; do not use it here.

Step 1 — Read the History page

The table has these columns: Date, Open, High, Low, Close, Adj Close, Volume.

  • Open = the first price of that day's bar. Close = the last price of it.
  • Use Close for everything in this challenge. Ignore Adj Close.
  • Read the row with the right date. Yahoo lists US trading dates, so Fri 2 Oct is the Fri 2 Oct row even though the day finishes early Saturday morning Singapore time. There are no rows for weekends.
  • Do not read the big live price at the top of the page. It moves during trading and is not the Close.

Step 2 — Work out the direction

You are predicting whether gold's Close on Fri 2 Oct will be above or below its Close on Fri 25 Sep.

If Close on Fri 2 Oct is……than Close on Fri 25 SepResult
higherUP
lowerDOWN
identicalVoid (no score either way)

Worked example (invented numbers, not real prices). Fri 25 Sep Close = 3,000.0. Fri 2 Oct Close = 3,040.0. 3,040.0 is higher than 3,000.0, so the answer is UP. Had it been 2,960.0, the answer would be DOWN. The size of the move does not matter, only the direction.

Step 3 — Make your call

  1. Record the Fri 25 Sep Close. That is the line gold has to cross.
  2. Read Mon 28 Sep and Tue 29 Sep. Is gold above or below the line so far? This is information, not the answer: a week can reverse.
  3. Ask what could push it from here, using your agents' work: the US dollar, interest-rate expectations, risk sentiment, and the chart trend. Then write UP or DOWN and the one-sentence reason. That reason goes to R5 for the Human Score.

The same page pattern for the two indices

Use the History pages for ^GSPC and ^NDX, and read Close the same way. For the indices you predict the actual Close level on Fri 2 Oct, to two decimals as Yahoo shows it, not just the direction.

Where each number goes

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Reading Adj Close instead of Close.
  • Comparing GC=F on one date with GCZ26.CMX on another.
  • Reading the live price at the top of the page instead of the row for the date.
  • Using the wrong Friday: baseline is Fri 25 Sep, target is Fri 2 Oct.
  • Writing "up" or "higher" in the file. It must read exactly "UP" or "DOWN", or the file is invalid.
  • Reading the Fri 2 Oct row before Yahoo has updated. Wait until after the US close and check again before posting.

2d · Evidence: no random predictions

Every call must be supported by three lenses: almanac, macro and technical. One sentence is not enough. Each team writes 9 evidence blocks (3 lenses × 3 instruments), each a paragraph of at least 60 words plus the specific fields below. They are part of predictions/vW4.json and are locked with it.

LensAuthorRequired fieldsWhat the paragraph must say
AlmanacR2pattern, sourceThe seasonal or calendar pattern that applies to this week, named with its source, and whether it supports the call. If the pattern is weak or contradicted by the other lenses, say so
MacroR2events (each with date, event, expected effect)The major scheduled or breaking economic news that could move this instrument this week, with dates, and how each is likely to push the price and why
TechnicalR3support, resistance, trendWhere support and resistance sit (as numbers), the current trend, and what a break above or below those levels would mean for the call

Technical: what "support and resistance" means

Support is a price area where falls have previously paused or reversed. Resistance is a price area where rises have previously stalled. Find them on the daily chart: recent swing lows and highs, last week's low and high, and moving averages such as the 20-day and 50-day. Support must be below resistance. Say which side of each level the price is on and what would change your mind.

Macro: what counts as "major news"

Scheduled releases (jobs, inflation, GDP, central-bank decisions and speeches) and any breaking event. Check every date on the official source, since schedules can change: the Federal Reserve (federalreserve.gov) for policy meetings and speeches, the US Bureau of Labor Statistics (bls.gov) for jobs and inflation releases, the US Bureau of Economic Analysis (bea.gov) for GDP and spending data. For gold, also cover the US dollar and interest-rate expectations. Typical relationships, not rules: higher-for-longer rate expectations tend to press on equities and on gold, and a weaker dollar tends to help gold. Explain your reasoning; do not just name the event.

Almanac: what to state

Use a named almanac or seasonal dataset. State the pattern, the source, and how the pattern applies to 28 Sep–2 Oct. Do not quote a pattern you cannot source.

Model paragraph (invented numbers, illustrating the level of detail only)

Technical, for an imaginary index: "The index closed Monday at 5,020, above its 20-day average of 4,985, and has been in an uptrend since mid-month. Support sits at 4,960, where it paused twice last week, with a second area near 4,900 at the 50-day average. Resistance is 5,060, the high it failed to clear on Thursday. A close above 5,060 would open room toward 5,100; a close below 4,960 would weaken the trend and argue against a higher finish. Volume has faded on the latest rallies, which favours caution over a large move."

R1's checklist before signing off

  1. All 9 blocks are present and every paragraph is a paragraph, not a sentence.
  2. Technical blocks give a numeric support below a numeric resistance.
  3. Macro blocks name dated events, and the dates were checked on an official source.
  4. Almanac blocks name a source.
  5. Each block names its author, and the author is a team member.
  6. The three lenses are consistent with the call, or the disagreement is stated openly.

What the validator checks: the form (fields present, 60-word minimum, support below resistance, sign-off present). What it cannot check: whether the reasoning is good. That is R1's sign-off, R5's Human Score, and your review after the lock.

3 · Preserving the integrity of the prediction

Cutoff: Tuesday 29 Sep 2026, 23:59:59 SGT (= 15:59:59 UTC)

  1. The clock is GitHub's, not the team's. A team's entry is the last push to main made at or before the cutoff, using GitHub's server-side push time. Commit timestamps are set by whoever commits, so they are not used.
  2. The lock job archives it, outside the teams' reach. It copies the file into dt3-monitor under locks/vW4/<team>.json together with the commit SHA, push time and a SHA-256 hash of the content. Every student has read-only access to dt3-monitor (I checked all 31 collaborators; only you have write), so no team can alter the archive.
  3. Scoring uses the archived copy only. Whatever happens to the team's file afterwards — edited, renamed, force-pushed — is irrelevant. A team that changes its file later simply has a file that no longer matches its locked hash.
  4. Hashes are announced. Right after the lock, the hash per team goes to Discord and onto a public ledger. A hash reveals nothing about the prediction, but anyone can verify their own.
  5. No entry means no entry. No file, an invalid file, or a push after the cutoff: 0 points and "NO ENTRY" on the leaderboard. There is no "I thought I submitted" — see the pre-lock status board below.

Pre-lock status board (participation lever)

At Tue 12:00, 20:00 and 23:00 SGT the monitor posts VALID / INVALID / MISSING for each team to Discord — status only, never the values. It also shows evidence completeness (blocks passing, out of 9) and whether R1 has signed off. This is the pressure that gets R1s chasing their teams before midnight, and it removes any excuse.

Two facts that shaped the design

  • The monitor's schedule cannot be the cutoff. dt3-monitor is set to run every 10 minutes, but its recent runs land 3–5 hours apart, and every one of the last 8 has ended in failure at the "Publish history to public site" step (the Discord half succeeds). So the cutoff is a time in the data, evaluated whenever the lock job runs, and the lock job is run manually on Wed morning rather than left to a 23:59 cron.
  • Whether teams can read each other's repos is unverified. All four team repos are private, but my token cannot see the org's base permission. If it is anything above "none", a team could read a rival's file before the cutoff. Check it before release. Fallback if needed: submit only the SHA-256 hash by Tuesday, and reveal the file after Friday's close.
