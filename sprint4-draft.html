2b · How to read the open and close — reliable sources

Use Yahoo Finance's daily History page. It is the source of record for all three instruments.

InstrumentHistory page
S&P 500 (^GSPC)finance.yahoo.com/quote/%5EGSPC/history/
Nasdaq-100 (^NDX)finance.yahoo.com/quote/%5ENDX/history/
Gold futures /GC, Dec 2026 (GCZ26.CMX)finance.yahoo.com/quote/GCZ26.CMX/history/
  1. Open the History page for the instrument.
  2. Set the time period to cover the dates you need, and the frequency to Daily.
  3. Find the row for the date. Read the Open and Close columns. Use Close, not Adj Close, for the prediction and for scoring.
  4. Record the value, the date, the page URL and the time you read it. If Yahoo's Download button is unavailable to you, reading the row on screen is enough.

Which dates to record

DateRecordUsed for
Fri 25 SepClose (all three)Baseline; the gold direction is measured against it
Mon 28 SepOpen and Close (all three)Agent inputs

Rules that keep it fair

  • One number of record. You read the Fri 2 Oct Close from Yahoo and post it by Sat 3 Oct 12:00 SGT (US close is early Sat 3 Oct SGT). That posted figure is the one everyone is scored on, even if Yahoo revises it afterwards.
  • "Friday close" means the New York close. Markets do not trade on Saturday, so Friday's Close is the last price of Friday's US session, which falls in the early hours of Saturday in Singapore: about 4:00 pm New York (04:00 SGT) for the two indices, and about 5:00 pm New York (05:00 SGT) for COMEX gold futures. For reference, the Fri 25 Sep S&P 500 Close is 7,743.41, the Sprint 3 target.
  • Same source for both dates. Gold direction compares two Closes from the same page. If Yahoo is unavailable, use Google Finance for SPX and NDX, and for gold use one alternative source for both dates. Never mix sources.
  • Futures caveat. COMEX gold trades almost around the clock, so its "Close" is the last price of Yahoo's daily bar and can differ from the official exchange settlement. That is why the source, the exact contract and both dates are fixed in advance.
  • R4 commits actuals/vW4.json (date, open, close, source URL, time read) for the dates above; the Fri 2 Oct value goes in by Sat 3 Oct 18:00 SGT. Stage 6 reads from it.
  • A tie on gold (identical Close on both dates) is void.

2c · Mini tutorial: reading the numbers and making your call

What is "gold futures (/GC)"?

A futures contract is an agreement to buy or sell something at a set price on a future date. /GC is the COMEX gold futures contract. Its price is quoted in US dollars per troy ounce. You do not trade it; you only read its price and predict which way it moves.

Decoding the symbol GCZ26.CMX

PartMeans
GCGold futures
ZDelivery month, December (futures month letters: G = Feb, J = Apr, M = Jun, Q = Aug, V = Oct, Z = Dec)
26Year 2026
.CMXThe COMEX exchange

Why a named contract? Every month has its own contract with its own price. If you compare a price from one contract with a price from another, the gap between them can look like a market move when it is not. So everyone uses GCZ26.CMX for both dates. Yahoo's GC=F is a continuous symbol for the same product; do not use it here.

Step 1 — Read the History page

The table has these columns: Date, Open, High, Low, Close, Adj Close, Volume.

  • Open = the first price of that day's bar. Close = the last price of it.
  • Use Close for everything in this challenge. Ignore Adj Close.
  • Read the row with the right date. Yahoo lists US trading dates, so Fri 2 Oct is the Fri 2 Oct row even though the day finishes early Saturday morning Singapore time. There are no rows for weekends.
  • Do not read the big live price at the top of the page. It moves during trading and is not the Close.

Step 2 — Work out the direction

You are predicting whether gold's Close on Fri 2 Oct will be above or below its Close on Fri 25 Sep.

If Close on Fri 2 Oct is……than Close on Fri 25 SepResult
higherUP
lowerDOWN
identicalVoid (no score either way)

Worked example (invented numbers, not real prices). Fri 25 Sep Close = 3,000.0. Fri 2 Oct Close = 3,040.0. 3,040.0 is higher than 3,000.0, so the answer is UP. Had it been 2,960.0, the answer would be DOWN. The size of the move does not matter, only the direction.

Step 3 — Make your call

  1. Record the Fri 25 Sep Close. That is the line gold has to cross.
  2. Read Mon 28 Sep and Tue 29 Sep. Is gold above or below the line so far? This is information, not the answer: a week can reverse.
  3. Ask what could push it from here, using your agents' work: the US dollar, interest-rate expectations, risk sentiment, and the chart trend. Then write UP or DOWN and the one-sentence reason. That reason goes to R5 for the Human Score.

The same page pattern for the two indices

Use the History pages for ^GSPC and ^NDX, and read Close the same way. For the indices you predict the actual Close level on Fri 2 Oct, to two decimals as Yahoo shows it, not just the direction.

Where each number goes

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Reading Adj Close instead of Close.
  • Comparing GC=F on one date with GCZ26.CMX on another.
  • Reading the live price at the top of the page instead of the row for the date.
  • Using the wrong Friday: baseline is Fri 25 Sep, target is Fri 2 Oct.
  • Writing "up" or "higher" in the file. It must read exactly "UP" or "DOWN", or the file is invalid.
  • Reading the Fri 2 Oct row before Yahoo has updated. Wait until after the US close and check again before posting.

3 · Preserving the integrity of the prediction

Cutoff: Wednesday 30 Sep 2026, 23:59:59 SGT (= 15:59:59 UTC)

  1. The clock is GitHub's, not the team's. A team's entry is the last push to main made at or before the cutoff, using GitHub's server-side push time. Commit timestamps are set by whoever commits, so they are not used.
  2. The lock job archives it, outside the teams' reach. It copies the file into dt3-monitor under locks/vW4/<team>.json together with the commit SHA, push time and a SHA-256 hash of the content. Every student has read-only access to dt3-monitor (I checked all 31 collaborators; only you have write), so no team can alter the archive.
  3. Scoring uses the archived copy only. Whatever happens to the team's file afterwards — edited, renamed, force-pushed — is irrelevant. A team that changes its file later simply has a file that no longer matches its locked hash.
  4. Hashes are announced. Right after the lock, the hash per team goes to Discord and onto a public ledger. A hash reveals nothing about the prediction, but anyone can verify their own.
  5. No entry means no entry. No file, an invalid file, or a push after the cutoff: 0 points and "NO ENTRY" on the leaderboard. There is no "I thought I submitted" — see the pre-lock check below.

Pre-lock check (participation lever)

During Wednesday, on request, staff run the check by hand and post VALID / INVALID / MISSING for each team to Discord — status only, never the values. It is not on a timer, because the monitor's schedule is unreliable. Teams should also self-check against R1's list: every call present, every reason present, gold reads exactly "UP" or "DOWN". This is the pressure that gets R1s chasing their teams before midnight and removes any "I thought I submitted".

Two facts that shaped the design

  • The monitor's schedule cannot be the cutoff. dt3-monitor is set to run every 10 minutes, but its recent runs land 3–5 hours apart, and every one of the last 8 has ended in failure at the "Publish history to public site" step (the Discord half succeeds). So the cutoff is a time in the data, evaluated whenever the lock job runs, and the lock job is run manually on Thursday morning rather than left to a 23:59 cron.
  • Whether teams can read each other's repos is unverified. All four team repos are private, but my token cannot see the org's base permission. If it is anything above "none", a team could read a rival's file before the cutoff. Check it before release. Fallback if needed: submit only the SHA-256 hash by Tuesday, and reveal the file after Friday's close.

Ways this could be gamed, and the answer

AttemptDefence

Delete or rename the file, re-create it later (Sprint 3 Team 1 pattern)Lock records what was present at the cutoff; a later re-creation cannot change it
