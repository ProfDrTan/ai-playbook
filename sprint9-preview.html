🏁 The Final Sprint Problem

"Sprint 9 is not the sprint where you finish building. It's the sprint where you prove, in front of me, that what you built actually works — and has worked for nine sprints, not just this one."

Sprint 8 was about hardening — making the pipeline survive without you. Sprint 9 is about something different: making sure every one of you can stand in this room on 3 August and show it, clearly and with command of your own work.

The single most important rule this sprint: no new features. If your team is tempted to add one more agent, one more data source, one more model this week — don't. Every hour goes into reliability and evidence. A team that polishes eight things that already work beats a team that half-finishes a ninth two days before Demo Day.

Sprint 9 exists to answer one question for every team: can you prove, live, that this system has been running and improving for nine sprints — and can every member of your team explain their part of it?

What Demo Day Evidence Looks Like — And Why "It Ran Once" Isn't Enough

Any team can point to a pipeline that produced output this week. That is not what separates a pass from a distinction on 3 August. The question is: what's the proof this has been a real system, not a one-off?

The Single-Run Trap — a Demo Day failure pattern

A team opens Demo Day by running the pipeline live, it produces a prediction, and they treat that as the whole story.

One successful run proves the code works today. It does not prove the system learned anything, survived any failure, or was ever running without someone watching it. That proof lives in your commit history, your tags, and your calibration table — not in a single live execution.

The Demo Day Evidence Test — what every team must be able to show

By Demo Day, your team should be able to answer yes to each of these, with a screen open to prove it:

• Can you show a github-actions[bot] commit from at least one week where no human touched the pipeline?
• Can you show a moment where an API call failed or was rate-limited, and the fallback handled it — not a human patch?
• Can you show the cumulative calibration table from Week 22 through Week 31 — not just this week's number?
• Can every team member explain, in their own words, the part of the system they own?

If the answer to any of these is no, that's what this week is for — not adding something new, but making one of these answers true.

What "ready" actually means

It doesn't mean a scripted performance. It means every person knows exactly which screen, which file, which commit they're pointing to when they say "here's the evidence" for their own part of the system. That clarity is what makes a presentation feel professional rather than improvised.

What Changes From Sprint 8
🆕 No new features — the one hard rule of this sprint

Sprint 8 was about hardening what exists. Sprint 9 is about proving it. Any team that spends this week adding scope instead of stabilising and preparing their evidence is working against their own Demo Day, not toward it.

🆕 One welcome exception: a frontend for your evidence

Several teams have asked whether they can build a frontend this sprint. Yes — and you're encouraged to. This is not the same as adding a new feature. A dashboard, a results page, a simple UI that presents your predictions and calibration history doesn't add new pipeline capability — it presents what your pipeline already does more clearly on Demo Day. If a page or two makes your evidence easier to see live in the room, build it.

One boundary: it has to sit on top of what already works, not become this week's main project. If your pipeline itself is still shaky, fix that first — a polished frontend on top of a fragile backend is worse than a plain terminal output on top of a solid one.

🆕 New: every role presents their own segment

Every role presents their own two-minute segment on Demo Day. This sprint, actually running that sequence out loud as a team — at least once, before Monday — is expected and will show in how the presentation goes.

🆕 New: the calibration table must be cumulative, W22–W31

Not just this week's delta. The full nine-sprint trend is the artefact that shows whether the system actually learned — it is the hardest thing to fake and the easiest thing to verify live.

Still in effect: mid-week check-in is a graded artefact

Sprint 9 Standing Reminders
The mid-week check-in is not a Discord message

Same rule as every sprint — R2 must contact each role member individually and document the result in standup_midweek_W31.md, naming each role with a status line. This is the last time it's assessed. Don't let it slip because it's the final week.

Tag format: vW31 — still enforced, no exceptions this time

Lowercase v, capital W, two digits. This is the final release tag of the term — get the format right the first time.

If something is still broken — fix reliability, don't add scope

If your pipeline still needs a manual step, or a piece isn't working, this week is for closing that gap — not for building something new around it. A smaller system that demonstrably works beats a bigger one you have to explain away on Demo Day.

The data domain is the training environment. Scrum is the lesson.

You were never assessed on whether you predicted the market correctly. You were assessed on whether your team ran a professional delivery cycle — planned, executed, reviewed, and retrospected — with a system that improved sprint over sprint. Sprint 9 and Demo Day are where that whole story gets told at once.
