Team Ultra — An Ideal Sprint 4 Submission

Team Ultra is not a real team. It's a worked example showing exactly what every part of the Sprint 4 brief looks like when it's done to the standard we're asking for — not the minimum, the target. Real numbers are used where one exists (Sprint 3's actual S&P 500 close); everything else — names, predictions, scores — is invented for the example. Open the live example dashboard →

How to use this page

Read each row below next to the matching panel on the example dashboard. The right column says what made it "ideal" rather than merely passing.

Roles

What's thereWhat makes it ideal
One row per sprint (vW3, vW4), the current sprint marked, a name in every one of R1–R6The history goes back further than the current sprint — it wasn't started only because this sprint required it. Every cell has a real name, not a blank or a placeholder.

GitHub Activity

What's thereWhat makes it ideal
Commits and last-commit time for all six roles, plus the ten latest commits by nameNo role shows zero. The "latest commits" list has messages that describe real work ("Add Roles and Predictions panels"), not "update" or "wip". Refreshed the same day it's viewed.

FigJam Activity

What's thereWhat makes it ideal
Cards spread across all four columns, each with an owner; every Done card has a Discord linkCards are not all sitting in Done — some are still To Do or In Progress, which is what a board looks like mid-sprint. Every column has at least one card from a role other than R1.

Locked Prediction

What's thereWhat makes it ideal
A call and a named owner for all three markets, one sentence per call, R1's confirmationEach sentence names something specific and checkable — a level, a dated pattern, a reason — not "I think it'll go up." That specificity is what lets the Monday defense actually work.

LLM Synthesis

What's thereWhat makes it ideal
All three models' views per market, whether they agree, a divergence note, and a narrativeThe example includes a real disagreement (DeepSeek splits from the other two on NDX) and says what was done about it, instead of three markets where all three models happen to agree.

Human Score

What's thereWhat makes it ideal
A score, a decision (confirm / adjust / override) and a justification for each marketNot every score is a 5, and not every decision is "confirm." The NDX score is a 3 with an "adjust," and the justification explains why in terms of what R6's synthesis actually showed — the Human Score is doing real work, not rubber-stamping.

Delta

What's thereWhat makes it ideal
Predicted vs. actual, the error and %, which agent pulled the call furthest off, and a narrativeUses the real Sprint 3 close (7,743.41) against an invented prediction, so the arithmetic is real and checkable. Names one specific agent as the biggest contributor to the miss, not a vague "several factors."

Prescription

What's thereWhat makes it ideal
One agent, one specific change, one reason, tied directly to the delta aboveIt's a single concrete change ("lean less on Almanac when it disagrees with the other two, and add a check"), not a general resolution like "be more careful next time."

Common ways teams fall short of this

  • Dashboard only appears at the end of the week, not kept live — the whole point of the Roles/GitHub/FigJam panels is to make the week visible as it happens.
  • One-sentence reasons that are too generic to question ("the market looks strong") rather than specific enough to defend.
  • Human Score that always agrees with the owner's number — a Human Score that never adjusts anything isn't doing its job.
  • Delta and Prescription disconnected from each other — the prescription should follow directly from what the delta found, not be a generic lesson.
  • FigJam board with every card already in Done, authored only by R1 — that reads as populated the night before, not worked through the week.
