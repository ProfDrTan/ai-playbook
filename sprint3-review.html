Sprint 3 — vW3 — Automate, and Show Your Work

Last week you did it by hand. This week two things change: at least one manual task gets automated, and — starting now — your team's actual Scrum process (backlog, Definition of Done, task status) has to be visible on the shared FigJam board, not just in your repo.

New, starting this sprint: FigJam is graded.

Sprint 1 and 2 didn't require board activity, and that wasn't penalized. From Sprint 3 onward, it is. This isn't decoration — it's how Scrum actually works: a backlog and a Definition of Done that only exist in someone's head, or buried in a GitHub file, aren't visible to your team or gradable as process. They have to live on the board.

The Sprint Goal

Automate at least one manual Sprint 2 task, and run your team's Scrum process through the shared FigJam board — backlog, Definition of Done, and task status all visible there.

The Backlog — All Six Roles, In Concert

1R1 — Product Owner & Scrum Master: run the board, log the room

Write your team's Sprint 3 backlog as individual cards in your team's To Do column on the shared FigJam board (not a duplicate — your section of the one master file). Each card needs a one-line Definition of Done. Move cards through To Do → In Progress → Review → Done as the week progresses. At Monday's presentation, log attendance and who spoke to which role.

DONE MEANS — a populated backlog on the board with DoD on every card, card positions that clearly moved during the week, and an attendance/rotation log from presentation day.
2R2 — Almanac & Macro: automate the seasonality/macro note

Script the lookup of at least one calendar/seasonality pattern or macro data point instead of finding it manually.

DONE MEANS — code committed that outputs one automated seasonality or macro note, plus a board card tracking this task through to Done.
3R3 — Technical & Economic: automate the OHLC pull

Write a script that fetches weekly OHLC for some or all of the 16 instruments from Sprint 2 via an API or library, instead of reading Yahoo Finance by hand.

DONE MEANS — running code, committed to your repo, outputting the same data for at least 5 of the 16 instruments, plus a board card tracking this task through to Done.
4R4 — Data & Calibration: validate the automation against the manual numbers

Take your team's Sprint 2 manual figures and your new Sprint 3 automated output for the same data, and check them against each other. Where they disagree, say why.

DONE MEANS — a short comparison (manual vs. automated, any discrepancies explained) committed to your repo, plus a board card tracking this task through to Done.
5R5 — Human Score & UI: build a live page, not a static one

Give a human score (1–5) to how much you'd trust this week's automated output vs. last week's manual output, with one sentence of reasoning. Then build a page that reads from R2/R3's automation output (a committed data file) rather than hand-typed numbers — if the automation reruns with new figures, the page should update without anyone touching the HTML. Include at least one chart (Chart.js via CDN, or hand-rolled SVG — numbers in a table alone isn't new this sprint), and show this week's automated figures side-by-side with last week's manual ones so the score has something concrete to react to.

DONE MEANS — a committed page that fetches from a committed data file (not hardcoded), renders at least one chart, and shows the Human Score + reasoning alongside a manual-vs-automated comparison — plus a board card tracking this task through to Done.
6R6 — LLM & GitHub: build it, ship it

Own the actual commit/PR for this sprint's automation code.

DONE MEANS — a clean commit or merged PR, plus a board card tracking this task through to Done.

Prediction Challenge — New This Sprint

Monday: your team commits one number. Predict this Friday's SPX closing price — a single figure, no range. Commit it to prediction_vW3.md in your repo by end of day Monday, timestamped by the commit itself so nobody can quietly edit it later.

Saturday morning (Singapore time), not Friday: the NYSE closes 4pm ET Friday, which lands around 4–5am Saturday SGT — so the real close isn't visible here until Saturday. Check then, compare your prediction to the actual close, and compute your error as a percentage: |predicted − actual| / actual × 100. Commit the result to the same file. Closest team wins bragging rights at Monday's Sprint Review — no grade attached, this one's just for fun and to make the automation feel real instead of academic.

Then: Write It Up and Present

Bring your working automation, your validation, and your live FigJam board to Monday's session. Be ready to show the board, not just describe it.
