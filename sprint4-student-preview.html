Sprint 4 — vW4 — Predict Three Markets, Defend Your Call

Last sprint you automated. This sprint your team makes a real call on three markets, locks it, and can explain it out loud. Then you build the last two stages of the system architecture: measuring how far off you were, and deciding what to change.

The Sprint Goal

Lock a three-market prediction by Wednesday night, defend it on Monday, then work out how far off you were (the delta) and decide one concrete change for next sprint (the prescription), and show the whole chain on your team dashboard. The dashboard also tracks your team's GitHub and FigJam activity, and it stays live and up to date all week.

Two New Ideas This Sprint: Delta and Prescription

These are Stages 6 and 7 of the system architecture. Nobody has built them yet, so here they are in plain words. In Scrum terms: your prediction is the sprint's work, the delta is your Sprint Review of it, and the prescription is your Retrospective action item.

Delta = the gap between what you predicted and what actually happened. It answers three questions:
  1. How far off were you? The size of the error.
  2. Was the direction right? Did you call up when it went down?
  3. Which agent pulled you furthest the wrong way? Look at what each of your agents (Almanac, Macro, Technical) and the LLMs said that week, and see which one pointed furthest from what happened and how much your final call leaned on it.
Prescription = the one change you make because of the delta. It is not a general reflection like "we should be more careful". It is a specific change to how you make next sprint's call. That usually means changing how much you lean on an agent (its "weight"), or adding a check to your process. You don't need any maths: "we lean less on the Almanac and more on Technical, because the Almanac was furthest off" counts. A number is optional.

Worked example (invented numbers, not real markets)

StepExample
What you predictedA market's closing level of 5,000, up from last Friday's close of 4,980
What actually happenedIt closed at 5,050
Delta: how far off50 points, which is about 1.0% of 5,050

How to read the close

The close is the close of market on Friday. Markets don't trade on Saturday, so Friday's close is the last price of Friday's New York session. That lands early Saturday morning in Singapore (stocks about 4:00 pm New York = 04:00 SGT; gold futures about 5:00 pm New York = 05:00 SGT). Check Saturday morning or later.

  1. Open the market's Historical Data page on Yahoo Finance, set to Daily.
  2. Find the row for the date.
  3. Read the Close column. Not Adj Close.

Source of record: Yahoo Finance, so every team is scored against the same number. If it's unreachable for the two indices, use Google Finance and say so in your commit message. For gold, use the same source for both Fridays.

Gold example (invented numbers): Friday 25 Sep close 3,000 and Friday 2 Oct close 3,040 means UP. The size of the move doesn't matter, only the direction. An identical close counts as no result. GCZ26.CMX means gold futures (GC), December (Z), 2026, on COMEX. Always use this exact contract for both Fridays, because a different contract month has a different price.

What counts as your entry

Whatever is on main at Wednesday 23:59 Singapore time, judged by when GitHub received your push (not the date on your commit). After that, the prediction is fixed.

Defend It on Monday

You must be able to defend your prediction in the practical on Monday 5 Oct. It's an oral conversation, not a written test, and it isn't marked. We take your one sentence and ask about the real thinking behind it: which agent output, which LLM view, what the Human Score decided, and why. Any team member can be asked, not just the owner of that market.
WeakGood (invented examples)
"I think the market will go up.""Support near 4,960 held twice last week and Friday's jobs report is the main risk, so I expect a modest rise."
"Gold will fall.""Rate-cut expectations were pushed out after the inflation data, which usually weighs on gold, and it has failed twice at its recent high, so I say DOWN."

A good sentence names something specific we can ask about: a level, a dated event, a pattern and where it came from. Keep the files behind it committed before the deadline (your R2 and R3 outputs, the LLM log, the Human Score) so you can open them on the day.

Your Dashboard: Your Live Weekly Tracker

Your dashboard is how your team's week is tracked. It must be live and up to date all week, not built at the end. We will open it at any point during the week. If a panel is empty or out of date, that is what we will ask about.

Style it however you like. The structure is the same for every team, so anyone can find what they're looking for.

  • It lives at ui/index.html in your team repo.
  • It has these seven panels, in this order, each tagged with the attribute shown.
  • Each panel reads its committed file directly. No hand-typed numbers.
  • Every panel shows Last updated, taken from the file's updated_at field, so nobody has to guess how fresh it is.
  • A panel whose data doesn't exist yet shows "Not yet available", not an error.
